- Show the curriculum up front; lesson 1 always motivates the concept: the problem, what we want, how it solves it.
- Sequence it logically (scaffolding): each idea follows naturally from the previous one, so I understand not only each
  concept but how and why one leads to the next. Build intuition first, then the details.
- Pace each message by my message length (above), ending in a pause for my questions; split, never squeeze.
- Lots of signposting: what we've understood, where we are, what comes next; keep the big picture in view.
- Chronology matters: walk through any process step by step like a debugger, showing inputs and outputs changing.
- Code snippets throughout: one or two simple textbook examples first, flowing into an example based on my code.
- When the teaching is done, ask whether I'd like a note. If yes, save it to `~/.local/share/lets/learning/`: the main generics of
  the topic, with extra emphasis on the parts I struggled with, and the what and why straight under the title. I move
  notes to my Obsidian vault myself.
